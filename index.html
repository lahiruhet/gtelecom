01The problem

It works. Until it doesn't.

Custom apps rarely fail all at once. Small things slip, and nobody is watching.

Nobody owns it any more.

The contractor or agency that built it has moved on. Your team uses the app every day. But keeping it running is nobody's job. So nobody does it.

Small failures go unnoticed.

A certificate expires. A dependency gets a critical security advisory. A deploy fails halfway. Each is a small fix on the day. Found by a customer six hours later, it's an outage.

A full-time hire is the wrong size.

Most months, a stable app needs a few hours of skilled attention. A full-time engineer means a full salary for that. They take time to hire. And when they're on leave, you're back where you started.

03How it works

How we take it over

Three steps. You keep ownership of everything. We take on the work.

  1. Step 1

    Access and audit

    You give us access to the repository, hosting, DNS and database. We review how the app is built, deployed and backed up. You get our findings in writing before we change anything.

  2. Step 2

    We instrument and take the queue

    We set up monitoring, alerting and backups, and fix what the audit flagged as urgent. From then on, alerts, security advisories and failed builds come to us first.

  3. Step 3

    Monthly report and a named contact

    Each month you get a written report. You also get a named contact who knows your app and answers your questions directly.

06FAQ

Questions before you book

Anything else, ask on the call or email us.

What access do you need?

Four things: your code repository, your hosting account, your DNS, and your database. We ask for the lowest level of access that lets us do the work.

Where the platform allows it, we use named accounts, not shared passwords. You can see who changed what, and remove our access at any time.

What happens if something breaks at 3am?

Monitoring alerts us, not your inbox. What happens next depends on the response window in your agreement.

If it includes out-of-hours cover, we start work within that window. If it covers business hours, we start at the beginning of the next business day. You choose which before you sign.

Either way, you get a written summary afterwards: what failed, what we did, and what we're changing so it doesn't happen again.

Who owns the code and infrastructure?

You do. The repository, hosting accounts, domains and data stay in your name, in accounts you control. We work inside them.

If any of it is still in your old agency's accounts, we'll help you move it into yours during onboarding.

What response times do you commit to?

The ones written into your agreement. We set your response window with you, based on how critical the app is to your business. The price reflects it.

We don't publish one number here. An online shop and an internal dashboard need different cover. Your monthly report lists each incident with when it was detected and when we responded, so you can check.

Will you work alongside our existing developer?

Yes. If a developer or agency still builds features for you, they keep doing that. We look after the app in production.

We agree up front who deploys what. Our patches go through the same staging check as their changes.

How do I cancel?

Email us with [NOTICE PERIOD] notice. Before we step back, you get a handover note: what's running, where the backups are, which alerts are set up, and which credentials to rotate.

Everything is already in your accounts, so there's nothing to migrate. We then remove our access and confirm it in writing.
